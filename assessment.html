The engagement

The AI Opportunity Assessment

What to do, when, and in what order.

What it is

We go inside the business. We interview the people doing the work, map the processes they run, review what has already been tried, and come back with a ranked schedule of initiatives: what each is worth, what it costs, and the order to do them in.

The output is a document written to be circulated to a board. It requires no platform and no data warehouse to act on.

How it runs

Interviews. Leadership first, then the people running the work in operations, sales and finance.

Process and systems. We map the recurring processes end to end and inventory the systems beneath them: what is licensed, what is used, and what people work around.

Prior attempts. Tools evaluated, experiments run, prototypes built by someone on the team. This is generally where the head start lies, and it is almost always underused.

Prioritization. Every opportunity scored on dollar return, cost and effort, and the organization's capacity to absorb it, then sorted into tiers.

What the document contains

The processes reviewed. Each recurring process, the tools in use, and the friction observed or already documented internally.

The opportunities. Every AI and automation opportunity identified, grouped by theme, each with an implementation sketch rather than a concept.

The prioritization matrix. Dollar return, cost and effort, and organizational capacity for each, sorted into tiers with the reasoning set out.

A sequenced plan. What to start with, what to pilot, who owns it, and what success looks like for each.

Where to go deeper. Which vendors merit evaluation, and where a prototype would settle a question faster than more analysis.

What we need

  • Time with each of the agreed leaders, and with anyone else management considers worth hearing.
  • Whatever process documentation exists: standard procedures, system overviews, templates, sample reports.
  • Examples of repetitive work — manual workarounds, communication templates, reporting rebuilt by hand each period.
  • An inventory of the software the business runs on.
  • Anything already attempted, including what did not work.
  • Availability for clarifying calls over the course of the review.

Broad system access is not required at the outset. Where reviewing live systems or data becomes useful, the level of access and the confidentiality terms are agreed first.

Confidentiality

Everything disclosed is confidential and used only for the assessment and for discussing what follows. Nothing appears in a case study, on a website or in conversation with any third party without written consent.

What follows

Many companies take the document and run it with their own people, and where that is the case we say so.

Where continuing makes sense, it takes one of three forms. A program direction retainer, under which one of the principals owns execution day to day. Implementation of the initiatives that matter most. Or, occasionally, capital, through OVD Capital. Each is a separate conversation and a separate agreement.

Fees for the assessment are fixed and agreed before work begins, scaled to scope rather than to the size of the company. We give you the figure on the first call.
