For founders

Twenty years of work should not be exposed to an experiment.

Most owners of good businesses have reached the same position: the technology plainly matters, there is no internal team capable of separating the useful from the noise, and nobody is prepared to bet the company on finding out. That is the gap OVD Capital works in.

Where it starts

First the value is established. Then both parties decide whether to pursue it.

Every relationship begins the same way: two to three weeks inside the business, concluding in a ranked schedule of where AI creates value here, what each opportunity is worth, what it costs, and the order in which the work should be done.

The document belongs to the owner. It can be run with an internal team, handed to an existing partner, or set aside. No obligation attaches to it and there is no automatic next step.

Where both parties agree on the figure, a second conversation is worth having: whether the owner wants a partner for the value not yet captured.

If it goes further

A second bite, not an exit.

Where the fit is right and both parties wish it, the principals invest. Typically OVD Capital takes a majority position and the founder retains a meaningful minority; arrangements have looked like 70/30 or 85/15, though every deal is negotiated on its own terms.

The founder takes real liquidity off the table, recognizing what has already been built, and keeps a stake in what follows — which is the value the preceding three weeks were spent identifying. Where the plan holds, the second bite can exceed the first.

What accompanies the capital

  • Funding for the work, without straining the operating business.
  • Two operators who have run companies through precisely this kind of change, and a third partner alongside them with thirty years of the same.
  • A plan that already exists, written with the management team before any transaction was discussed.

What OVD Capital is, stated plainly

The principals invest their own capital, deal by deal, alongside partners they have worked with for twenty years. There is no fund behind them and no committed pool awaiting deployment. For most founders this is an advantage: no ten-year clock, no pressure from limited partners, and no forced exit because a vintage is closing. It should nonetheless be understood at the outset, and it is a fair question to ask directly.

Fit

Where this tends to work.

The businesses in which AI repays fastest share a shape. People spend substantial hours on work that repeats; knowledge resides with a few senior people rather than in any system; and the same information is re-entered across three or four applications. Services firms, distribution, light manufacturing, healthcare, financial services and multi-unit operations.

Profitable, founder-owned, generally $1MM or more in EBITDA, with real customers and proven unit economics — businesses that are sound rather than broken, and constrained by infrastructure rather than by demand.

Where that is not the case, OVD Capital will say so on the first call rather than after an engagement.
