Services

Three service lines.

Most clients begin with an assessment and continue into build work. Each is scoped and priced on its own, and none of them commits a company to the next.

AI Opportunity Assessment

A ranked schedule of the AI initiatives a business should fund, what each returns in dollars, what each costs to complete, and the order to take them in. Delivered as a document written to be circulated to a board.

The assessment covers interviews with leadership and with the people doing the work, a map of the recurring processes and the systems beneath them, and a review of what the company has already attempted internally.

Fixed scope, fixed fee. Many companies take the document and run it with their own people.

Implementation

Building the initiatives that matter. Retrieval systems over a company's own documents and history, agent systems built against internal and public data, structured capture of work that is currently unrecorded, reporting that replaces hand-built spreadsheets, and automation of the coordination that consumes operating capacity.

Work runs on the systems a business already owns wherever that is possible. Migration is the most expensive purchase available to a mid-market company, and it is rarely the right place to start.

Scoped per initiative, with a documented handover that leaves no continuing dependency.

Program direction

A single external owner of execution, responsible for the pilots, the tooling, vendor selection and management, and reporting on a fixed cadence to the board and to management.

This exists because the most common reason an AI program stalls is that it lands on people who already hold full-time roles. Program direction continues until each initiative is live and a named person inside the company has taken it over.

Retained monthly.

Fees

Assessments are fixed-fee, scaled to scope rather than to the size of the company, and agreed before work begins. Implementation is scoped per initiative. Program direction is a monthly retainer.
