The hard part

The technology isn't the difficulty. Knowing what to do and what it returns is.

Every board has asked and every management team has said yes. The decision was made a while ago and it wasn't hard.

What's missing is a defensible answer to a narrower question. Of the dozen things this company could do, which two or three are worth doing — and what does each one actually return, in months rather than someday?

That is a judgment about the business: where the hours go, which work repeats, what a point of margin is worth here. Not a judgment about the technology. It needs someone who has carried a P&L, which is most of why the answers you have been given so far haven't been useful.

What we answer

Three questions, in this order.

First

What to do

Not everywhere AI could apply. The two or three places in this business where it pays, ranked against each other, with the reasoning shown so you can argue with it — and the ones we'd tell you to leave alone.

Second

What it returns

What each initiative is worth and how long before it shows up — weeks, a quarter, or a year, and honestly which. Anything measured at eighteen months has nobody accountable for it and usually never arrives.

Third

How to build it

The sequence, what to buy versus build, where a human stays in the loop, and who owns it. This is where most plans stall — so we can drive it too, until it's running and someone inside has taken it over.

Usually the answer involves no new software at all. Migration is the most expensive thing a mid-market company can buy, and an AI layer over the systems already in place pays back in weeks rather than years.

How engagements run

Start small, then stay until it works.

  1. 01 AI Opportunity Assessment Interviews, process mapping, a ranked opportunity matrix, and a ninety-day path. Fixed scope, fixed fee. 2–3 weeks
  2. 02 Program driver One outside owner of execution — runs the pilots, builds or configures the tools, manages the vendors, reports on a fixed cadence. Part-time and consistent beats full-time and episodic. Monthly
  3. 03 Implementation Build the two or three things that matter, with the people to do it. Documented handoff, no dependency on us. Per initiative
  4. 04 Capital Where the fit is right and both sides want it, we invest our own money alongside partners. Deal by deal — never the stated purpose of an engagement. If it fits
