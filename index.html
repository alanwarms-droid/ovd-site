What operating experience changes

Familiarity with what these tools can do is now widely held. Judgment about which of them will pay inside a particular business is not, and that judgment comes from having run companies.

It shows up in four places: knowing what survives contact with a working operation rather than a demonstration; estimating the real cost of an initiative once integration, exceptions and adoption are counted; insisting that a process be sound before anything is built on top of it; and separating what is genuinely true about this technology from what is true only in a demonstration.

How initiatives are ranked

  • Dollar return What the initiative is worth in the accounts, discounted to today. Hours saved are converted to dollars or they are not counted.
  • Cost and effort The whole cost, not the licensing line: integration nobody scoped, the exceptions that turn out to be half the volume, training, and the stretch before anything runs properly.
  • Organizational capacity What the company can absorb at once. Three initiatives run well finish ahead of nine run badly.

The same method in every business, so the answers hold up against each other. For a fund, that means opportunities rank across the whole portfolio rather than within a single company.

Who we work with

Private equity firms, across the companies they own, where the same method applied in each holding makes the results comparable across a book.
