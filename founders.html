For founders

You built a real business. AI should not be something that happens to it.

You know the technology matters. You also are not going to bet twenty years of work on an experiment, and you do not have an AI team to sort the useful from the noise. That is the gap we work in.

Start here

First we establish what it is worth. Then we decide together whether to pursue it.

Every relationship starts the same way: two to three weeks inside the business, ending in a ranked list of where AI creates value here — what each opportunity is worth, what it costs, and the order to do them in.

The document is yours. Run it with your own team, hand it to an existing partner, or act on none of it. There is no obligation attached and no automatic next step.

If we are both right about the number, there is a second conversation worth having: whether you want a partner for the value that has not yet been captured.

If we go further

A second bite, not an exit.

Where the fit is right and both sides want it, we invest. Typically we take a majority position and you keep a meaningful minority — arrangements have looked like 70/30 or 85/15, though every deal is negotiated on its own terms.

You take meaningful liquidity off the table, recognizing what you have already built, and retain a stake in what follows — the value we have just spent three weeks mapping. Where the plan works, the second bite can exceed the first.

What we bring besides money

  • Capital to fund the work without straining operations.
  • Two operators who have run companies through exactly this kind of change — and one more partner alongside us with thirty years of the same.
  • A plan that already exists, because we wrote it with your team before anyone talked about a transaction.

Being straight about what we are

We invest our own capital, deal by deal, alongside partners we have worked with for twenty years. There is no fund behind us and no committed pool waiting to be deployed. For most founders that is an advantage: no ten-year clock, no LP pressure, and no forced exit because a vintage is closing. You should know it going in, and you should ask us about it directly.

Fit

Where this tends to work.

The businesses where AI pays back fastest share a shape: people spend real hours on work that repeats, knowledge lives in a few senior heads rather than in a system, and the same information gets re-entered across three or four tools. Services firms, distribution, light manufacturing, healthcare, financial services, multi-unit operations.

Profitable, founder-owned, roughly $1MM or more in EBITDA, with genuine customers and proven unit economics — businesses that are good rather than broken, and constrained by infrastructure rather than demand.

Where that is not the case, we will say so on the first call rather than after an engagement.
