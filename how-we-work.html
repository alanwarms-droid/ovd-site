How we work

What to build, what it returns, and who builds it.

The same method in every business, so the answers hold up against each other.

Where companies get stuck

Most management teams can name a dozen things they could do. What is missing is the ranking: which of the twelve is worth funding first, what each is worth in dollars, and how much the company can take on at once without breaking something that works.

How initiatives are ranked

  • Dollar return What the initiative is worth in the accounts, discounted to today, so that timing sits inside the ranking rather than beside it. Hours saved are converted to dollars or they are not counted.
  • Cost and effort The whole cost, not the licensing line. Integration nobody scoped, the exceptions that turn out to be half the volume, the training, and the stretch before anything runs properly.
  • Organizational capacity What the company can absorb at once, given who is available and what else is underway. Three initiatives run well finish ahead of nine run badly.

Scored the same way each time. For a fund, that means opportunities rank across the whole portfolio rather than within a single company.

Make or buy

AI has made it an order of magnitude easier to automate a business process. It is easier to build something bespoke, and it is simultaneously easier for software vendors to advance their own roadmaps. Both sides of the decision moved at once, which is why a familiar question feels like a new one.

Building is cheaper than it was, though it still takes a build mindset and a plan to maintain what gets built. What it offers is the avoidance of a migration: surgical strikes to capture value where it sits, rather than a project to uproot the systems the business runs on.

Buying brings support, certainty and a roadmap shaped by many customers, along with integration effort and lock-in that is difficult to reverse.

The answer turns on cost, benefit, organizational capability, and alignment with what the business is defensible at.

Then we build it

Where nobody inside has the hours, we run the work ourselves: the pilots, the tooling, the vendors, and reporting on a fixed cadence. We stay until each initiative is live and a named person inside the company has taken it over.

Where a company can run it with its own people, we say so.
