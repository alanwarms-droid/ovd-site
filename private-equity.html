For private equity

Portfolio companies rarely lack conviction. They lack selection and capacity.

OVD Capital establishes which initiatives a portfolio company can realistically execute, what each returns and over what interval, and delivers them where the company has no capacity of its own. The method is identical in each engagement, so that findings compare across the book.

The problem

Conviction was the easy part.

Nobody in a portfolio requires persuading. The investment committee has asked, limited partners raise it at every annual meeting, and each management team has responded — with a workshop, a pilot, a licensing decision, a slide bearing four workstreams. A year later the candid answer to what changed in the accounts is frequently nothing.

Two things are absent, and the second is the more consequential. A company confronting a dozen plausible options has no reliable basis on which to choose among them, nor to state what any of them is worth. Management proposes what management wishes to build; vendors propose what vendors sell; operating partners are generalists asked to adjudicate a technical question; and the consultancies produce a maturity model.

Then, once the schedule is correct, the hours do not exist. Mid-market portfolio companies are lean by design. The chief financial officer holds three roles, there is no internal lead and no budget line for one, and an operating partner covers twenty companies. A sound plan is handed to people who already carry full-time responsibilities, and it stops there.

The scarce judgment is knowing which of twelve credible options merits a quarter of somebody's attention, and what it returns. The scarce resource, right after that, is anyone with the hours to build it.

Selection criteria

What moves an initiative to the top of the schedule.

Six tests, applied to every candidate. Those that pass are undertaken first; those failing two are usually a year away from mattering.

  • Repetition rather than invention The same judgment exercised hundreds of times a month, by people, from information already held within the business. Work recurring twice a quarter does not repay the effort of automating it.
  • Data that exists and is already trusted A plan opening with the cleansing of data is a data project. Those run for a year and are usually canceled before completion.
  • Human review preserved The initiatives that ship are those in which the system drafts and a person approves. Full autonomy is where pilots stall, and where the liability resides.
  • Compatibility with existing systems A CRM or ERP replacement moves payback from weeks to years and is generally lost to change management well before then. Migration is the most expensive purchase available to a mid-market company.
  • Existing internal demand Adoption is the binding constraint, not capability. An initiative nobody has asked for does not ship, however strong its business case.
  • Measurable within a quarter Where success is defined at eighteen months, no one is accountable for it and it rarely arrives.
Common failures

Four ways a sound program loses a year.

Licenses distributed without a change to the work. Most portfolios have already purchased seats for everyone and observed no movement. This is not a tooling problem and additional seats will not resolve it. The work itself must change shape, which is a management task rather than a purchase.

Client-facing deployment in relationship businesses. Where the relationship constitutes the product, clients should experience the results of the technology — speed, precision, recall — and never its machinery. This is the most common way a portfolio company damages itself with good intentions.

Infrastructure ahead of application. A warehouse is occasionally necessary and almost never the place to begin. It frequently serves to defer the harder conversation about what would be done with it.

Platform replacement with capability attached. Vendors are extending the systems these companies already operate. Riding an established roadmap captures most of the value of new software at none of the migration cost. Purchase where the vendors are close; construct only where the advantage is proprietary.

Across the portfolio

The value compounds on repetition.

A single assessment tells a management team what to undertake. The same assessment conducted in three or four companies establishes something of greater use to a fund: where the operating upside is concentrated, which opportunities recur and might therefore be constructed once, and which management teams are capable of execution.

Because the method and scoring are identical in each instance, the outputs are directly comparable. Opportunities rank across the book rather than within a single holding, and capital and attention follow the return rather than the most insistent chief executive.

Conduct of an engagement

  • Two to three weeks per company: interviews with leadership and with those performing the work, a process and systems map, and a review of what has already been attempted.
  • Each opportunity scored on value, cost, complexity, risk and time to impact, then tiered, with the reasoning set out so that it may be examined.
  • A ninety-day plan with named owners and a single success measure for each initiative.
  • Fixed fee and fixed timetable, agreed in advance.

What follows

The work continues until it is running.

The arrangement that succeeds is a single external program director, part-time but continuous, responsible for the pilots, the construction or configuration of tooling, vendor management, and reporting on a fixed cadence to the fund and to the chief executive. Part-time and continuous is more effective than full-time and episodic, and both are preferable to a plan delivered to a team without the capacity to act on it.

Direction continues until each initiative is live and a named person within the company has assumed it. Where a portfolio company is capable of running the work itself, OVD Capital will say so and hand it across; that is the exception rather than the assumption.

Co-investment

Where the principals regard a business sufficiently highly, they will commit their own capital, and are equally willing to serve as operating partner on a deal of the fund's own. Investment is made deal by deal, with no fund and no clock. OVD Capital does not compete with institutional buyers; its own investing sits well below that scale.

Diligence on a live deal

The firm occasionally examines a target during diligence, assessing whether the narrative presented is substantiated and what operating upside is genuinely available following completion. It is the same work against a shorter timetable, and is available on request, though it is not what most funds engage OVD Capital to do.
