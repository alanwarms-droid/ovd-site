On a live deal or inside a company you already own. We have run the businesses, carried the P&L, and sat on the boards — which is why we can tell a genuine operating opportunity from a good demo.

The problem

AI is on every investment committee agenda and almost nobody has a defensible answer.

Sellers have learned to put an AI narrative in the CIM. Management teams have learned to present one. Neither is necessarily lying — but the distance between a working prototype used by three people and a durable operating advantage is enormous, and it does not show up in a data room.

Meanwhile the specialists you might call are technologists. They can tell you whether the architecture is sound. They cannot tell you whether the margin is real, whether the team will adopt it, or whether the whole thing is a feature the incumbent software vendor ships next year for free.

That is the judgement we sell. Not what AI can do — what it will do, in this company, given these people and these systems, on a timescale that matters to your hold period.
